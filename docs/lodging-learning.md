# Learning: recording where you stay, honestly

19 November 2026. Each destination with at least one night now has a **Where you're staying** panel in the day view. The traveler records a name, an optional address or note, check-in and check-out times, and whether they have booked it themselves. They can type a name or use **Find stays nearby**, which lists up to 12 mapped hotels, guesthouses, hostels, apartments, motels and chalets within 5 km of the destination centre. A stay with a location shows as a blue house-shaped pin on the day map.

## What it is and is not

It is the traveler's own record. It is **not** a booking, a price, an availability check or a review, and nothing contacts a supplier. The wording says so: "marked as booked by you", "Prices, availability and booking status are not checked". A name-only stay is valid; it just has no map pin. The map search is a partial OpenStreetMap category search, not a list of every hotel, and many OSM names are short or generic (the live Jaipur search returned names such as "Fly" and "Jaipur").

## Decisions and tradeoffs

- **No migration.** The record is an optional `lodging` object inside the existing per-stay entry in `preferences.stay_preferences`, keyed by the stop's logical id. That path is already validated, copied between plan versions, frozen in reviews and returned by the API, so the feature inherits version, ownership and autosave behaviour without a new table. The cost: it is not queryable by SQL and not a normalized ledger. If stays later need dates per night, several stays in one destination or linked costs, that is the point to promote it to a table.
- **Reuse the place search.** `lodging` is a sixth category in the existing activity discovery (Geoapify `accommodation`, or OSM `tourism=hotel|guest_house|hostel|apartment|motel|chalet` for the Overpass fallback), so it keeps the same caching, rate limits, retention and attribution. The activity pickers list their categories explicitly, so lodging never appears as an activity.
- **A searched place keeps its source.** The stay stores the provider id, coordinates and source text. Editing the name of a searched stay drops the location unless the name is unchanged, so a renamed place never keeps the old pin.
- **Validation twice.** The backend `Lodging` model and the frontend `isLodging` agree: name 1-120 plain characters, address up to 200, `HH:MM` times, both coordinates or neither, (0, 0) rejected, a provider id requires coordinates, unknown fields rejected. The frontend also accepts the `null`s the server returns for unset fields.
- **Not part of the visit line.** The stay pin is unnumbered and is never joined by the dashed line; the numbered line stays the visits. It does widen the map bounds so it is never off screen.
- **An import cycle.** `day-map.ts` takes only a type from `lodging.ts`, because `lodging.ts` imports `validCoordinates` from it and the unit-test loader resolves imports recursively.

## From the stay to the first visit (20 November 2026)

A recorded stay with a map location can now answer the first question of the day: when do I have to leave? Under the stay panel, a **Getting from your stay to <first visit>** disclosure takes a mode (walk or drive without live traffic) and an arrival buffer, calls `POST /api/v1/journeys/from-stay` and shows "Leave by HH:MM to reach <visit> at HH:MM" with the minutes, distance, route warnings and the Geoapify/OpenStreetMap credit.

- **A separate endpoint, not a loosened one.** `/journeys/check` takes two retained place ids and a gap between two activities; a stay is neither a retained place nor does it have an end time. The new request takes the stay's coordinates (range-checked, (0, 0) rejected, NaN/inf rejected), a retained destination place id, mode, local start time and buffer. It returns the leave-by time, or `needs_earlier_start` when the visit starts too early for the route (leave-by would be before midnight). Same Geoapify route call, rate limit and `private, no-store`; nothing is stored.
- **First visit** is the earliest non-skipped activity of the day. Both ends need coordinates; unmapped visits or stays say so and never get a name-geocoded guess. Changing the stay, mode, buffer, or the visit's time/place discards the result.
- **Verification.** Automated: 12 new backend tests (leave-by arithmetic including the midnight boundary, 7 invalid inputs, unknown place 404, sign-in 401, no database writes) and 3 frontend tests; 561 backend and 204 frontend tests pass; typecheck and build pass; OpenAPI snapshot regenerated. Live, signed in (local web and API, hosted database, QA trip `77d6c6b1-ec94-4653-ba83-2d4fa8d41101`): with ITC Rajputana as the stay and Amrapali Museum at 10:00, walking returned "Leave by 09:24" (26 min + 10 min buffer, 1.6 km) and driving "Leave by 09:44" (6 min, 4.1 km); changing the mode cleared the old result; at 390 px there was no overflow and no control under 44 px. The QA trip was returned to its baseline (visit removed, stay removed) and a reload confirmed it.
- **Several visits (live, signed in, same QA trip):** with a stay and four visits (two at 10:00, two at 13:00) the check named the earliest visit, breaking the 10:00 tie by id exactly as the timeline does; the map showed the blue stay pin plus numbered visits 1-4 with the status "4 mapped visits shown; your recorded stay is marked". All four visits and the stay were then removed and a reload confirmed the baseline. This closes the earlier gap about a stay beside numbered visits.
- **Not verified:** a screenshot (the preview window was hidden for most of the run), the hosted app (not pushed), and a first visit before 00:30.

### The way back (20 November 2026, later)

The same section now also answers the evening question: from the day's **last** visit (latest end, skipped ones ignored) back to the stay. `POST /api/v1/journeys/to-stay` takes the stay coordinates, a retained origin place, mode and the visit's end time, and returns the arrival time ("Leaving Jaipur Wax Museum at 18:00, you would be back at your stay by 19:20") or `after_midnight` when arrival would pass 24:00. A visit that itself runs past midnight has no end clock time, so the UI says to split it instead of calling the API. No buffer applies on the way back. Seconds in the end time round up. Results for the two legs sit side by side; changing the mode or buffer clears both.

Verified: 10 more backend tests (arrival arithmetic around 23:59, bad input, 404, 401, no writes; 571 pass in total) and 3 more frontend tests (207 pass); build passes. Live, signed in, same QA trip, stay ITC Rajputana with Albert Hall 09:00 and Jaipur Wax Museum 17:00: out walking "Leave by 07:50" (60 min + 10 buffer, 3.9 km), back walking "back by 19:20" (80 min, 5.2 km), back driving "18:27", both results visible together, no overflow or short controls at 390 px; a first version cleared the outbound result when the return check ran, which was fixed. Baseline restored and confirmed by reload. Not verified: hosted app, screenshot. Not built: the return journey to the stay, and using the stay as the origin of the existing pair checks.

### The AI day planner knows the stay (20 November 2026, later)

The Groq day planner now receives the recorded stay for the day's destination as plain evidence: name, address, check-in/out and whether it has a map location, never the raw coordinates. When the stay has a location, each mapped candidate also carries `distance_from_stay_km`, a straight-line distance rounded to 0.1 km (computed server-side from retained place records), and the prompt tells the model to prefer nearer candidates, leave time to get back, and never claim or confirm a booking. The stay text is treated as untrusted, like every traveler label. Validation is unchanged: the model can still add only catalog ids, the extra key is ignored by the checks, and the accept boundary rebuilds the catalog without it.

- **Straight-line, not a route.** The distance is a hint, labeled so in the prompt; real journey times still come from the explicit checks.
- **Why not coordinates?** The model does not need them and they are the traveler's lodging location; distances give it what it needs.
- **Verification.** Automated: 4 new backend tests (stay appears in the model context without coordinates; absent without a stay; distances only for a located stay, about 10 km for a 0.09 degree shift; the stay helper returns None without a stay) and a full run of 574 passing. Live, signed in, with a real Groq call and a local worker, on the QA trip: with ITC Rajputana recorded, "Generate day proposal" completed and returned five sensible blocks (two museums, a park, a meal break, free time) inside 09:00-18:00 with the standard unverified-evidence notices. I cannot see inside the model's choice, so this shows the path works, not that the proposal is closer to the stay. The proposal was discarded and the stay removed; a reload confirmed the baseline and that the session was still signed in.
- **Not verified:** that the model measurably prefers nearer places (an evaluation across several runs would be needed), and the hosted worker.

### The chat summary and day explanation mention the stay (20 November 2026, later)

"Summarize my route" appends the recorded stay to each destination ("2 stay nights (staying at Hotel Aurora, marked as booked by you; your own record, not confirmed with the stay)") and "Explain this day" ends with "You are staying at Hotel Aurora; ...". These answers are built on the server from the saved plan; the model only routes the request, so the stay text never goes to Groq and cannot be used as an instruction. A stay without the booked flag never says "booked".

Verified: 2 new backend tests (576 pass in total); live, signed in, through the chat with a real Groq routing call on the QA trip: both answers showed the stay, with the booked flag when set. The stay was then removed, a reload confirmed the baseline and that the session was still signed in. The chat messages themselves remain in the QA trip's conversation history (messages are not plan data and the app has no delete). Not covered: the hosted app; the chat's "stays" intent (changing a stay still answers "unsupported").

### Review follow-up (20 November 2026)

A code review of the stay commits found two real defects, now fixed: the stay-journey component kept an old result when a visit's duration changed (its reset key left out the duration, so the way-back headline paired a stale route with a new end time), and the distance code assumed coordinates were always present (it now skips the distance unless both stay and place coordinates exist). Also: the stay line on the "Explain this day" answer is now added after the 2,900-character cut so the disclaimer is never truncated, and the rounding policy of the two journey endpoints is documented (leave-by truncates seconds, arrival rounds up; both are the safe side). While fixing these I found the distance test described above had never been saved; it now exists, plus a half-coordinate case and a start with seconds. The full backend suite is 578 passing and the frontend 207.

### Check-in and check-out caveats on the route checks (20 November 2026, later)

The stay route checks assume the traveler starts the day at the stay and ends it there. That is not true on the arrival day before check-in or on the departure day after check-out, so the section now says so instead of presenting a confident "leave by". On the arrival day, when the first visit starts before the check-in time (or the last visit ends at or before it), it adds: "Check-in is from 14:00, so you may not be at your stay yet. This estimate assumes you start from it." (or "return to it"). On the departure day, when a visit starts at or after the check-out time: "You check out at 11:00, before this visit starts, so you may have left your stay by then. ..." Days in between, and stays without those times, show nothing. The estimate itself is not changed or hidden; the note only qualifies it. The rule is a pure function compared as zero-padded HH:MM strings.

Verified: 1 new frontend test covering every branch (208 pass); build passes. Live, signed in, on the QA trip: with check-in 14:00 and a 10:00 visit on day 1 both notes appeared, and I reworded the way-back note after reading it in the browser ("assumes you return to it"). Baseline restored and confirmed by a reload, session kept. The departure-day branch was then also checked live on day 3 with check-out 11:00 and a 12:00 visit (both notes appeared), and removed again with the baseline confirmed by a reload. Not verified: the hosted app.
